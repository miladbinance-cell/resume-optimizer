## 📱 Reddit — r/resumes

**Title:** I've reviewed 1000+ resumes. Here's the #1 mistake 90% of you are making.

**Body:**

You're using the SAME resume for EVERY job application.

Here's why that kills your chances:

ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) scan your resume for keywords from the job description. If your resume doesn't have those exact words, you get filtered out. Automatically. Before a human ever sees it.

Example:
- Job description says: "Experience with microservices, Kubernetes, CI/CD"
- Your resume says: "Managed backend services and deployments"

ATS Result: REJECTED. Why? None of the keywords matched.

The fix: For each job you apply to, find the top 10 keywords in the job description and naturally work them into your resume.

**Bad:** "Managed backend services and deployments"
**Good:** "Led migration of 5 backend services to microservices on Kubernetes, building CI/CD pipelines that reduced deploy time by 40%"

Same experience. Different words. Different result.

---

📝 **Other common mistakes:**

1. **No numbers.** "Managed a team" → "Led a team of 12, delivering 15 projects on time." Every bullet point needs a number.

2. **Weak verbs.** "Responsible for" → "Led." "Helped with" → "Built." "Worked on" → "Designed and implemented."

3. **Fancy formatting.** Tables, columns, graphics = ATS nightmare. Keep it clean, plain text, standard headings.

4. **One-size-fits-all.** You can't send the same resume to 100 jobs and expect results. Each job has different keywords.
